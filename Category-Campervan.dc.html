Campervan

This is Rhivannon. I built her in 2020 to be able to make thrifty, boomerang-style trips out and back after big busy landscape installation seasons - I would leave late in the summer when everyone's getting heat exhausted and it's time to take it easy until the worst of the heat passes.

I say boomerang-style because my work was all so local / not remote that I would squirrel away my travel nuts in the busy Spring installation season and go as far as I could in July/August, then make it back in time to start taking my daughter to school again.

In retrospect, I think the best way to do campervan travel is with remote work or more of a budget so you're not rushing past all of the amazing scenes along the way. There's so much to see! Turns out, there are also not so many places to camp (turns out I'm extremely particular about where I'll sleep, even in a gloriously custom turtle shell of my own making).

Through this experience of building a completely off-grid capable van that could take me as far away from people as I wanted to get - I learned that it's actually kind of creepy when I'm completely isolated. I don't like it. It's not the solo time I have a problem with, I'm just an urban person!

As I realized this, I also realized that I only really like heavily peopled, nicely maintained campgrounds. So bourgeois this was that this + gas + all van costs almost equated to paying rent at my inner-city apartment. And god forbid I have to sleep parked on a street where cars would be flying by my head - van life requires more agility in where you end up staying at night.

My pull back to urban areas didn't include many camp-able areas, just a lot of learning about what makes a driveway perfect for the night. All this said, I'm infinitely grateful I got to have this build experience and all of trips that came of it.

I sold the van to a really special young couple that had every intention of going out into the mountains and being completely unreachable, and I love that for them.

I had the van listed for a couple of months before they found it and they were brilliant enough to hire a local inspector to check it out before they drove in from Florida.

This older gentleman with decades of RV travel experience looked my van up and down with notes and photos for about seven hours and at any point I was ready for him to say, "how has this van not exploded yet?!" Surprisingly, he ended the assessment by saying I did a good job, and that I had it listed for a good price.

The couple was there the next day and for the next few months I got photos of my build in the mountains with their two small dogs. I hope it held up well for them, that potential modifications were easy, and amazing trips were afforded and as magical as they could've possibly been.
